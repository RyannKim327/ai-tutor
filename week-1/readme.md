### What is a Collection?
In programming, a 'Collection' is just a way to group several pieces of information together in a single container. Imagine a grocery list, a playlist of songs, or a deck of cards. Instead of having ten separate notes for ten different items, you have one single list that holds them all.

### The Concept: Finding the King of the Hill
Our goal this week is to learn how to look through a group of numbers and pick out the **largest** one. 

Think about it: if I handed you a stack of 50 cards, each with a different number on it, how would you find the highest number? You wouldn't be able to see them all at once! Instead, you would look at the first card, remember its value, then look at the second card. If the second card is bigger, you forget the first one and remember the second. You keep going through the whole stack, always keeping the 'biggest one seen so far' in your head. By the time you reach the bottom, the number in your head must be the biggest one in the whole stack!

### Why This Matters in Programming
This logic is everywhere! 
- **Social Media**: Finding the post with the most 'likes' in a feed.
- **E-commerce**: Sorting products from most expensive to least expensive.
- **Gaming**: Finding the player with the highest score in a match.

### The Weekly Challenge: The High-Score Hunter
Imagine you are building a simple scoreboard for a video game. You have a list of scores from a recent tournament. Your mission is to write out the logic to find the **highest score** in that list.

**Your Input:** A list of numbers (e.g., `[45, 82, 19, 94, 33]`).
**Your Goal:** Identify the single largest number in that list (e.g., `94`).

### Thinking Process & Strategy
Before you worry about how to write it, think about the *steps* your brain takes:
1. **Start small**: If I only gave you two numbers, you'd just pick the bigger one. 
2. **Build up**: If I gave you a thousand, you'd need a 'emory' to hold the highest number found so far.
3. **The Comparison**: Every time you see a new number, you perform a simple 'Yes/No' test: *Is this new number bigger than what I already remember?*
