### The Concept: Making a Choice

In programming, a **decision** happens when we ask a question that can only be answered with a "Yes" or a "No" (often called a True or False question). 

If the answer is "Yes," we follow one path. If the answer is "No," we follow a different path. This ability to branch off into different directions is what allows a program to react differently depending on what is happening.

### Real-Life Analogy: The VIP Guest List

Imagine you are the bouncer at a very exclusive club. You have a specific rule: **"Only people wearing blue shirts can enter."**

1. A guest walks up wearing a **blue** shirt. You look at them, check your rule, and say: "Welcome in!"
2. A guest walks up wearing a **red** shirt. You look at them, check your rule, and say: "Sorry, you cannot enter."

You aren't doing math or counting; you are simply comparing what you *see* (the shirt color) against your *rule* (must be blue) and choosing a reaction.

### Why This Matters in Programming

Almost every app you use is built on these decisions. 
- *Is the password correct?* (Yes $\rightarrow$ Log in | No $\rightarrow$ Show error)
- *Is the battery low?* (Yes $\rightarrow$ Dim screen | No $\rightarrow$ Stay bright)
- *Is there enough money in the account?* (Yes $\rightarrow$ Approve purchase | No $\rightarrow$ Deny transaction)

### The Weekly Challenge: The Quality Control Bot

You have been hired by a candy factory! The factory has a machine that produces colorful jellybeans, but some of them are the wrong color. Your job is to design the logic for a "Quality Control Bot" that inspects jellybeans.

**Your Mission:**
Create a logical process that takes a single jellybean color as input. 
1. If the jellybean is **Green**, the bot should say: "Pass: This is a perfect green jellybean!"
2. If the jellybean is **ANY OTHER color**, the bot should say: "Reject: This is not the color we need!"

### Thinking Process & Strategy

Before you start, walk through it in your head:
1. **Input**: What information are we receiving? (The color of the jellybean)
2. **The Rule**: What is the exact condition we are looking for? (Color must be Green)
3. **The Outcome**: What happens if the rule is met? What happens if it is not?
